# Gate A4: audit of the vault, the watch exchange, the parsers and the boundaries

**The largest review this project has run, and the one that changed how it reviews.** Four scopes,
three independent models, twenty rounds, eleven closed-question verification briefs, and a closing
round that was scored rather than filed. 133 findings, three of them high.

**The findings themselves are not here.** `docs/audits/A4-board.md` carries all 133 in the words
they were filed in, the arc of the counts, and the standing panels. Each round's returns and
analysis are in their own files. **This document is what the gate concluded**, including what it
failed at and what it left open on purpose.

## What was reviewed

| Scope | Subject | Rounds | Findings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | the vault at rest | 5, plus verification briefs | 40 |
| 2 | the watch key exchange | 7 | 27 |
| 3 | the parsers | 3 | 21 |
| 4 | the boundaries: app group, share extension, URL schemes, WatchConnectivity | 5, plus verification briefs | 45 |

**The scope with the most findings is not the most dangerous one.** Scope 4 ran to forty five
because the boundary surface is wide and much of it is prose. Scope 1 produced the highs that would
have cost somebody their accounts.

## Where it ended

**Nothing is open at any severity.** Four items are settled by decision rather than by code.

| Item | State |
| --- | --- |
| S1-33 | **waived**, with what would reopen it: iCloud replacing bytes at the same primary key between a read and a write |
| S1-40 | **waived**, with what would reopen it: the disable path moving accounts before the refusal, on an arrival |
| S1-37, S1-39 | **withdrawn**, and the withdrawal is unanimously confirmed |
| S4-22 | **void**, retracted by the reviewer who filed it |

All three reviewers accepted both waivers as shipping decisions. One of them supplied the sentence
the S1-40 waiver now carries: accepting it is not the same as the toggle being trustworthy under
twins.

## The three findings that mattered most

**S1-1: the wrapped key never synced**, so a phone that turned sync on sent every account to iCloud
and kept the only means of reading them on one device. Replacing that phone would have lost every
account silently. Fixed, and then measured on two devices in `E8` and `E9`.

**S1-6 and S4-1**: creation overwriting a record that arrived during key derivation, and the lock
and snapshot cover protecting only one window scene, so a second scene showed live codes.

**None of these were found by the tests that existed.** That is the point of the gate and it is
also the beginning of what it learned.

## What the gate learned about its own method

### Reviewing fixes generates work, and that is the instrument working

Round one found 44 items. Reviewing those fixes produced roughly 40 more, and reviewing those
produced fewer again. **Four rounds rejected a fix outright, three of them unanimously**, and every
one of those rejected fixes had a passing test written by whoever wrote the fix. The mtime clamp
passed a test that could not see the attack. The creation check passed a test whose fake never
changed. The consent timer passed a test asking at 121 seconds when the timer asks at 120.

**On 19 August the board went backwards**: from eight open and none above low, to eleven open and
three medium, with roughly eight of the eleven being defects in that day's own fixes. That is the
worst moment in the gate's record and it is the most useful one.

### The working rules that came out of it

**Tests are written from the finding's words before the fix exists.** **Every fix is reverted once
to watch its own test go red**, because a test that passes both ways is not a test. **Decisions are
extracted out of app targets no test can reach**, which produced `WatchProvisioningFlow`,
`AppLockPresentation`, `ProvisioningDesk`, `WatchInbox`, `WrappedRecordStore`, `ImportLimits`,
`JSONSniff`, `BoundedFile`, `InboxDirectory` and `EraseGate`. **A class sweep beats an instance
fix**, repeatedly: one pairing rule lived at five call sites and had drifted at two, and one
description of App Lock lived in nine sentences across six files and survived three partial
corrections.

### The closed question returns an answer; the open question returns work

**Eleven verification briefs ran on top of the review rounds and none produced a backlog.** The
format is one finding, the change in front of it, and a yes or no. Three of those briefs ended two
to one, and each time the dissent was specific and each time it was right.

**The instrument decides what comes back.** That is the second finding about method, and it shaped
the closing round.

## The correction that matters more than any finding

**S1-37 was not real, and the work done in its name deleted coverage that had been running all
along.**

One reviewer reported that twenty six Keychain tests executed in no job and on no machine. Two
facts behind it were true: `swift test` skips them, and the hosted job passes `-only-testing`. The
third, that the package test group was not compiled into the hosted target, was inferred and never
run. It was false. The target's `fileSystemSynchronizedGroups` had listed both groups since the
project's first Xcode commit.

**All three reviewers verified the finding as fixed.** It became a commit message and a CI rule.
The fix deleted a Keychain availability probe that was quietly carrying fifteen executions, so the
repair removed real coverage and manufactured a second finding, S1-39. Both were withdrawn, and the
withdrawal was confirmed unanimously after the topology was measured rather than argued.

**The class has a name now and it recurred four times in one day across three reviewers**: a static
check standing in for a dynamic one. Something checkable by running it gets settled by reading it,
and the reading is correct about every fact it names while being wrong about the one it does not.

**`docs/MASVS.md`'s evidence rule is the response**: a verdict that cannot name something a reader
can execute or read does not get to be a pass.

## The exit checks, and the reply round

**The closing round was two instruments stapled together**, and it is worth separating them because
they behaved completely differently.

**Three closed questions** (who supplied the S1-37 premise, are the two waivers acceptable, is the
MASVS self-assessment believable) behaved like the eleven verification briefs. All three reviewers
placed the premise correctly, accepted both waivers, and believed the published fail and partials
while refusing to treat a pass as independent assurance.

**One open question** (would you trust this) produced a reflex rather than a reading. Asked what
they would recommend instead, all three named a hardware token, which cannot do the thing they were
asked about, because it has no passphrase recovery path.

**The open half raised nineteen objections and every one was checked.** That produced a second
round, put to the same three reviewers as closed questions with the platform facts cited so they
could be checked rather than accepted.

### What checking them found

**One objection was live and worse than described.** `WatchProvisioning.respond` is overloaded, and
each overload generates its own ephemeral phone key. The freshness test called the raw bytes
overload; the app calls the validated one. **A static phone keypair on the shipping path passed all
457 tests.** Measured by applying the mutation, not inferred. That is the fifth instance of the
class named above, and the first where an overload hid it rather than a seam.

**Four objections did not survive measurement.** The absence of update enforcement, the vault key's
protection and the compromised-device scope each overstated the exposure, and half of the crypto
coverage objection was already closed. **In three of those cases this project's own wording invited
the error**, which is a new direction of failure here: the habit of stating limits sharply
overshooting into conceding things that are not true.

**And the reply round found three factual errors in this project's own documents**, because it
handed reviewers citations and told them to check rather than accept. A Settings path that iOS had
moved. A count of shipping binaries that did not match the script. And a claim that wrapping the
vault key under a Secure Enclave key would harden nothing on an unlocked device, which is false: an
Enclave key with a `.userPresence` access control requires a check on every use.

**Two MASVS verdicts were taken down** by two reviewers independently, MASVS-AUTH-3 and
MASVS-PLATFORM-1, both from pass to partial. **The self-assessment was too generous in the
direction that flatters the author**, which is worth more than the verdicts the sample confirmed.

## What this gate leaves open, deliberately

**The unlocked-device posture.** All three reviewers reached it by three different routes. The
vault key is a Complete Protection file the process can read whenever the phone is unlocked, and
daily use never re-derives it from the passphrase. App Lock is a gate in front of the interface
rather than in front of the key, and it is off by default. **The remedy is named and costed in
`docs/MASVS.md` under MASVS-CRYPTO-2** and it is not built. It is a product decision rather than a
defect, and it belongs to the roadmap and to gate A5 rather than to this gate.

**A blocking item that shipped unproven.** `docs/VAULT.md` lists WatchConnectivity routing
exclusivity on a list of things to prove before implementation. Half of it was measured in `E5`. The
other half, a rogue watch app claiming to be this app's counterpart, has never been run. A decision
was recorded not to run it, with the reasoning labelled as reasoning and the remedy written down.
**Two of the three reviewers rejected that decision**, and their objection is recorded in the
document it is about.

**Two waivers**, each with what would reopen it, and each accepted by all three reviewers as a
shipping decision rather than a safe window.

**One measurement nobody has made that costs an afternoon.** Whether other iOS authenticators leak
the same app switcher window. Two opposite claims are on the record with no evidence behind either.
Written up in `docs/APP_LOCK.md`.

## What this gate does not establish

**It is not a professional audit and it does not replace one.** Three language models and one
developer, and this document has just described the models agreeing confidently on something false.
`README.md` carries that warning and it stays until it stops being true.

**It says nothing about a released binary.** `docs/BUILD_PROVENANCE.md` narrows the gap and does
not close it. Apple re-signs what it distributes, so no locally computed hash reaches a device.

**It is a snapshot.** Gate A5 audits the diff of every release for exactly this reason: audited in
2026 says nothing about the build shipping two years later.

**And the reviewers' own verdict is not that this is safe.** None of the three would make it their
only holder of a second factor. `README.md` now says the same thing in the project's own voice,
before any reviewer says it: generate an encrypted export, check that it imports, and keep it
somewhere this app does not touch.
